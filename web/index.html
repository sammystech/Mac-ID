What you get

Fast, private, and honest about its limits.

Built on Apple's Vision and Core ML frameworks, with a face-recognition model that runs on the Neural Engine in under ten milliseconds.

Nothing leaves your Mac

Camera frames are processed in memory and never written to disk. Your face data and your password never touch a network — there is no server to talk to.

Recognises you, not a lookalike

A ResNet-100 face model measured at 100% recognition on both clean and partially covered faces, with zero false accepts across 54,978 impostor comparisons.

Rejects printed photos

A printed photo held to the camera makes a moiré pattern that skin does not. Mac ID looks for it, alongside screen glare and device edges, and refuses to unlock.

Ready before you are

The camera starts warming the instant your Mac locks, so recognition begins as the notch finishes opening rather than after it.

Glasses, light, angle

Enrolment captures nine head positions, and matching accepts whichever of them you actually resemble — so a different expression or a change of light doesn't lock you out.

Touch ID guards the key

Your password is encrypted with a key that only your fingerprint can unwrap. It is asked for once per launch, never at the lock screen.

How it works

Four steps, about two minutes.

Install and grant access

Drag Mac ID to Applications. It asks for the camera, and for Accessibility so it can type your password at the lock screen.

Enrol your face

Follow the ring: look straight ahead, then turn slightly in each direction. Mac ID stores a mathematical description of your face — not a photograph.

Paste your licence

Settings → About → paste the key from your purchase email, and press Activate.

Lock your Mac

The notch opens, finds you, and types your password. If it doesn't recognise you, nothing happens and you type it yourself.

Already using Mac ID?

Version 1.9 moved to a new, Apple-verified signature, and copies from before it can’t update to it on their own. Download it once from this page and drag it into Applications to replace the old one. Your licence and settings come across automatically. You’ll set up your face and password again, and approve Camera and Accessibility once more, because macOS ties those to the app’s signature.

Worth knowing before you install

Mac ID is a convenience feature, not a security upgrade. A MacBook camera sees a flat image, where an iPhone builds a 3D map of your face — so this cannot be as strong as Face ID or Touch ID. It reliably refuses a printed photo and a photo on a phone screen; it does not reliably refuse a video of you.

macOS gives no way for an app to authorise a login, so Mac ID unlocks by typing your stored password. That password is encrypted on your Mac behind Touch ID. If you are not comfortable with that trade, this is not the app for you.
