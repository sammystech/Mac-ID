3. What Mac ID is, and what it isn't

Mac ID recognises your face with your Mac's camera and, when it does, types your stored macOS password to unlock your Mac. Please understand exactly what that means:

  • It is not as secure as your password, Touch ID or Apple's Face ID. It uses an ordinary camera, which sees a flat image rather than a 3D map of your face.
  • It can be fooled. Mac ID tries to reject printed photos and photos shown on a phone screen, but it does not reliably reject a video of you, and no face recognition is perfect. Someone who looks very like you, such as a close relative, may be recognised as you.
  • Anyone Mac ID recognises can unlock your Mac. That includes anyone who can put you, or an image of you, in front of the camera — including while you're asleep, restrained or not paying attention.
  • It stores your Mac password. The password is encrypted on your Mac, and the key that decrypts it is released only after Touch ID or your Mac password. It is never sent to us or anyone else. Even so, storing a password always carries some risk.
  • It isn't suitable for every Mac. Don't use Mac ID on a Mac that holds information you're legally or professionally required to protect, a Mac other people share, or any Mac where an unwanted unlock could cause serious harm.
