6. What Mac ID is, and its limits

Mac ID is a convenience feature. It recognises a face with your Mac's camera and, when it does, types your stored macOS password to unlock your Mac. It is not a security product, and it is not a substitute for your password, FileVault, Touch ID or any other security measure. You understand and agree that:

  • Mac ID uses an ordinary camera, which sees a flat image rather than a 3D map of your face. It is not as secure as your password, Touch ID or Apple's Face ID.
  • Mac ID can be fooled. It may be unlocked by a photo, a video, a screen, a mask, a model, a close relative, a twin, someone who looks like you, or any other person or object it mistakes for you. Liveness detection and eye contact reduce some of these risks but do not remove them.
  • Anyone Mac ID recognises can unlock your Mac. That includes anyone who can put you, or an image of you, in front of the camera, including while you are asleep, unconscious, restrained, distracted or unaware.
  • Mac ID may fail to recognise you, or refuse you, for example because of lighting, glasses, a hat, a mask, makeup, injury, ageing or a change in your appearance, or because of the camera, macOS or your Mac. When that happens you type your password yourself.
  • Mac ID unlocks by typing your password through macOS Accessibility. It could type into the wrong place, type when you didn't intend it, or fail to type at all.
  • Mac ID stores your macOS password on your Mac, encrypted. Storing a password anywhere always carries risk, and no encryption is guaranteed to be unbreakable.
  • Mac ID depends on features of macOS and your hardware that Apple can change or remove at any time, which may stop Mac ID working in part or entirely.
  • Mac ID may contain errors, bugs or security vulnerabilities.
